Why an Iris Classifier?

The iris dataset is a cornerstone of machine learning — 150 flowers, 3 species, 4 measurements. But interacting with it in a console is not intuitive.

This Shiny app lets anyone — even without R experience — classify a flower by simply moving sliders:

  • Adjust sepal length, sepal width, petal length, and petal width
  • Pick the number of neighbors (k) for the kNN algorithm
  • Get an instant prediction with confidence score and visual comparison

The Algorithm: k-Nearest Neighbors

test_flower <- data.frame(Sepal.Length = 5.8, Sepal.Width = 3.0,
                          Petal.Length = 4.0, Petal.Width = 1.2)
pred <- knn(iris[,1:4], test_flower, iris$Species, k = 5, prob = TRUE)
cat("Predicted species:", as.character(pred),
    "\nConfidence:", round(attr(pred, "prob") * 100), "%")
Predicted species: versicolor 
Confidence: 100 %

The model computes Euclidean distances from the new flower to all 150 training points, then takes a majority vote of the k closest neighbors.

How Separable Are the Species?

Petal dimensions alone separate the three species well — setosa is clearly distinct, while versicolor and virginica overlap slightly.
